# Collaborating

*[Français](../fr/collaborer.md)*

All the collaborators of a project work on the same text at the same time;
each sees the others' cursors. Feuillet's server keeps the project, so
collaborators need not be online at the same time.

## Sharing a project

The **Share** button, at the top of the editor, opens **Share Project**.

### Inviting people

Under **Invite collaborators**, give the name of a Feuillet account or an
email address, and choose a role:

- **Can edit**: changes the project as you do;
- **Can review**: comments and suggests changes, always tracked, that the
  others accept or reject (see [Review](review.md));
- **Read-only**: reads and compiles the project, without changing it.

An account invited by name sees the project appear in its list. For an
email address, Feuillet sends a personal invitation itself, as Overleaf does,
if your account email is confirmed (see [Account](account.md)); otherwise it
opens your email program with the invitation. The link works once, for 30
days, and you may take it back before; **Resend** sends it again.

Only the project's owner invites. A project belongs to whoever created it;
for an older project, **Become the owner** claims it.

The owner can hand the project over, as on Overleaf: **Make owner**, next to
a member in **Share**. That member becomes the owner, and the former owner
stays on as an editor, who may then leave it.

### Sharing with a link

**Share with a link** gives links that anyone can open, without an
invitation: one for editing, one for review and one read-only. Under **Take
back access given by a link**, the owner replaces the editing link or the
read-only link with a new one: the old one stops working. Invited people
follow the change by themselves.

### Asking for more access

As on Overleaf, whoever reads a project, by a read-only link or as a reader,
may ask its owner to review or edit it, and a reviewer to edit it: **Ask for
access**, next to « Read-only » in the header, opens the **Share** dialog,
with **Ask to review** and **Ask to edit**. You need an account, signed in.
The owner is told by email and by a number on their **Share** button; in
the dialog, **Access requests** lists who asks for what, with **Grant** and
**Decline**. Granted, the person becomes a member with that role, and the
project is in their list under its new link, once; they are told by email,
and the header offers **Open it for editing**. Declined, they keep the
access they had, and are told too. Until the owner decides, the request can
be withdrawn from the dialog.

### Removing someone, leaving a project

In the list of members, **Remove** takes a person off: the link of their role
is replaced, and the project leaves their list.

A project shared with you can be left: **Leave project**, on its card in
the project list, or from the trash. It leaves your list on all your
devices.

## Seeing who works where

The avatars of the people present show next to the project's name, in the
editor's toolbar and next to files others have open. Clicking someone's
avatar opens the document they are in and scrolls to their cursor; their
line lights up for a moment in their color.

## The project's collaborators

The **Collaborators** tab (the people icon, on the left, next to **Files**)
shows:

- **Here now**: the people present, with the file each one is in; clicking
  that file goes to their cursor;
- **People of the project**: the owner, the accounts invited with their role
  (edit, review, read), and those who came by a sharing link, with what that
  link allows. **Refresh** reads the list again.

The project's people are shown to its members, and to whoever edits or
reviews it by a link; not to whoever reads it by the read-only link. Someone
who opens a link without being signed in to an account is not named: they
only show under **Here now**, while they are there. **Share**, at the top of
the tab, opens the sharing dialog, where the owner invites and removes
people.

## Chatting

**Project Chat**, at the bottom right, is a chat of the project's own, kept
with it.
